The Long Road Back (to Myself)

By Douglas Cirillo · First published on Substack, August 26, 2025 · Site edition, November 2025

What did a good life on paper hide?

In my mid-to-late 20s, I had what looked like a good life.

A tight group of friends. A girlfriend in the mix. A growing career.

We worked hard, played hard, made memories.

From the outside, it probably looked ideal.

But I was messing a lot of it up.

Not loudly. Not all at once.

But over time, through bad decisions, selfish impulses, and failing to take stock of the impact I had on people around me.

Why did I run?

I grew up bullied and introverted.

Later, I got in shape. Found confidence. Found attention.

And I leaned into that attention, maybe too much.

Instead of looking inward, I ran.

I left town. Found a new job. Started over.

At the time, it felt like the cleanest option. A reset.

And to a degree, it worked.

I built a career. I met incredible people. I met the woman who would become my wife and the mother of my kids.

Does the past disappear?

But the past doesn’t disappear.

Especially in the New York metro area, where the world is smaller than you think.

Over time, I reconnected. Slowly, cautiously. One friend at a time.

Not everyone welcomed me back. I didn’t expect them to.

But some did. And those moments, whether just for closure or something more lasting, meant a lot.

They taught me something:

Being honest about who you are, including your failures, is more powerful than trying to be perfect.

What changed when I showed up differently?

I started showing up differently.

Not as a polished version of myself, but as the full story. The flaws. The regrets. The growth.

That didn’t fix everything. But it did build something better.

It made me more present, with friends, with my wife, with my kids, with myself.

During COVID, my wife and I left the city and ended up back near where I grew up.

One town away.

It felt full circle.

Like life had brought me back to a place I needed to face, not just geographically, but emotionally.

What carries into the work?

Now, in my early 40s, I’m still unpacking that.

Still learning how to be real.

Still figuring out how to sit with past mistakes instead of outrunning them.

Still trying to deepen the relationships that matter, by showing up fully, not selectively.

And here’s where this ties to everything I’ve learned in work too:

  • Building systems requires honesty about where they’re broken
  • Momentum comes from small steps, not from pretending everything is flawless
  • Real growth, whether in organizations or relationships, comes from clarity, ownership, and trust

What is the takeaway?

I don’t have a neat ending to this one.

It’s more confession than lesson.

But if there’s a takeaway, it’s this:

Authenticity won’t protect you from pain.

But it gives you a chance to rebuild.

Not by erasing the past.

But by owning it, and growing from it.

Thanks for reading,
Doug
