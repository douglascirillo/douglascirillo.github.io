Own Your Harness

By Douglas Cirillo · First published on Substack, November 8, 2026 · Site edition, November 2025

What does owning the harness look like?

Guillermo Rauch put it sharply last week: own your harness, your data, your runtime. His version is about local models and open source. Mine is smaller and closer to home. What does owning the harness actually look like when it has to hold a working life, and then a brand?

Start with the shopping problem. Everyone I talk to is shopping for a better model. New release drops, benchmarks get posted, group chats light up, and somebody starts a migration plan by Friday. I think most of them are shopping for the wrong thing.

Here is my own setup, because it is the simplest version I know. I run a good part of my working life through an AI assistant. What makes it useful is not only the model. It is the harness around the model, and the harness is mine. There is a short document that says how it should behave and what it should never do. There is a memory, kept in files I own, of what matters to me and how I like things done. There are goals it works toward when I am not in the room, with logs I can read afterward. And there are approval gates, enforced in the system rather than requested in a prompt: a short list of things it cannot do without asking me first, no matter how confident it sounds. A prompt is a request. A gate is a control.

The model underneath all of that is swappable, and swapping is the point. The rules carry over. The memory carries over. The gates carry over. The performance does not, not automatically: a new model has a different tone, different judgment, different failure modes. So you re-test. That is not a flaw in the approach. That is the approach.

Where do the wins actually come from?

That is my Builder OS in miniature, and it is the same pattern I have watched play out at every brand I have run digital for. The wins never came from whichever platform we happened to be renting that year. They came from the layer we owned: the customer data foundation, the content model, the martech running on them, and the measurement discipline that tells you whether any of it worked. Vendors changed. Suites got acquired, roadmaps got rewritten. The foundation stayed ours, so every change on top of it was an upgrade instead of a migration.

A monolithic platform is the opposite bet. It is somebody else’s harness, rented. Their context, their guardrails, their idea of what your customer is. It demos beautifully, because a harness always does when the person who built it is driving. Then your edge cases show up, and you find out which parts you are allowed to change.

What is the Monday test?

Agents make this urgent in a way the platform era did not. An agent acting on bad context is not a rounding error, it is a behavior. So before I trust any agent setup, mine included, I run a short check. I call it the Monday test, because Monday is when you find out what your systems did over the weekend.

One: Brand Out-of-Bounds. Ask the agent for something outside its remit and see what it does. A good harness pivots back to what it can actually help with, cleanly, without inventing things.

Two: Contextual Switch. Change the intent mid-session. New constraint, new goal, drop the old parameters. A good harness drops them. A bad one keeps quietly serving the first version of you it met.

Three: Guardrails. Push on it with the requests it should never say yes to. A good harness holds the line, the same way every time. The polite wording can vary. The gate cannot, because the gate is enforced, not suggested.

Three tests, ten minutes, and you know whether the setup is worth trusting this week. Then you run it again after every model or vendor change, because that is when harnesses quietly become rentals.

Why let the engines compete?

Models are going to keep changing, at least every quarter and often faster. That is not a problem to solve, it is weather. If you own the harness, every new model is a better engine in a car that already handles the way you like, and you still retune it and re-test it. If you rent the harness, every new model is a move, and you do not even own the boxes.

Build the harness. Own the harness. Let the engines compete for the privilege of running in it.
