How did a parade turn into Atlantic City?

There’s one St. Patrick’s Day I still think about more than I probably should.

It wasn’t planned. It wasn’t smart.

But it’s one of the best memories I have, and it all started at work.

Two of my close friends and colleagues, let’s call them “Dan” and “Chris”, decided we’d kick off the Morristown, NJ parade with “kegs and eggs” at a local bar.

The day got loud fast. Guinness. Jameson. Floats. Chaos.

Somewhere along the way, a fraternity brother texted about a party at his condo overlooking the street. We went. Dan bailed early. Chris and I stuck around, then grabbed lunch and crashed at my place in Chatham to recover.

And then came the text.

“You home?”

Yeah.

“Be there in 5. Get your shit together. We’re going to AC.”

No plan. No hotel. Just three friends in a car heading to Atlantic City, running on impulse and momentum.

On the ride down, Chris, half-buzzed, fully philosophical, started riffing.

Random thoughts. Rants. Ridiculous one-liners. Dan handed me a notebook, The Ledger, and told me to write everything down.

I wish I still had it.

It was absurd and brilliant in equal measure.

We pulled into the Tropicana, hit the tables, drank too much, danced at the club (Chris may or may not have gotten kicked out for dancing on a table, at a woman’s request), and then passed out in the car just before sunrise.

The next morning, we saw a strip club advertising a “FREE BUFFET.”

We were curious, sure, but not that curious.

We turned around and went to Dunkin’.

Still one of the best decisions I’ve ever made.

Why does it still matter?

This wasn’t just a wild story.

It was a memory. A friendship forged outside of deadlines and decks. A reminder that some of the most important people in your life enter through the side door, colleagues who become something more.

We didn’t build that bond over Zoom.

We didn’t trade memes in Slack and call it culture.

We worked together.

Shared space. Shared pressure. Shared wins.

We showed up for each other in the ways that matter.

And years later, that’s what I miss most in remote work.

Not just the energy, but the unexpected connection. The kind that sneaks up on you between meetings, after launches, during “want to grab a drink?” moments that turn into lifelong stories.

What is the thread that runs through it?

This post might read like a tangent.

But it ties everything together.

It’s about the human part of this job, the piece that doesn’t live in your tech stack or org chart. The part that makes the work worth it.

Because the truth is:

  • Remote work can be isolating.
  • Transformation dies without trust.
  • And belief? Real belief? That comes from doing it together.

The AC ledger isn’t just a running joke or memory.

It’s a symbol of what happens when you let go a little.

When you follow the current.

When you say yes to the people around you.

No roadmap. No approval flow.

Just trust, a little risk, and the kind of day you never forget.

Thanks for reading,
Doug
