SEO, GEO, and AEO Are Just UX Done Right

People still introduce me as the SEO guy. I stopped shrugging it off. At Sotheby’s, usefulness, not hacks, drove organic growth of 167%. At A Place for Mom, mapping intent to pathways did the work keywords never could. This essay first ran on Substack in September 2025. The acronyms have multiplied since. The fundamentals have not moved.

By Douglas Cirillo · First published on Substack, September 16, 2025 · Site edition, November 2026

Why is good SEO just good UX?

Let’s get this out of the way: SEO isn’t about gaming Google anymore. Hasn’t been for years.

But people still think of it as a bolt-on practice. A dashboard metric. A set of hacks.

The truth is simpler: good SEO is just good UX.

If you help users get what they want in the most direct, intuitive, and performant way possible, you’ll win in organic search. Full stop.

At Sotheby’s, I broke it down like this:

Product (Design) + Technology (Performance) + Content (Subject Matter) = User Experience (SEO)

That’s it. That’s the formula.

Google is not just a search engine. It’s the largest consumer insight and discovery platform in the world. People come with intent. They want answers. They want confidence. Your job is to meet them there.

That’s not marketing. That’s product.

Where is the proof?

At Sotheby’s, we optimized artist pages not for clicks, but for usefulness. It drove registrations, bids, and consignments. One user found an artist page, clicked through, and ended up consigning a million-dollar piece. No ad spend. No direct outreach. Just clarity and design in service of need.

At A Place for Mom, we didn’t chase keywords. We mapped user intent to pathways. Families looking for assisted living vs. memory care had different questions and levels of urgency. We built content and flows around that. Conversions went up. Call volume increased. Care specialists were more effective.

That’s SEO. Not a silo, but the byproduct of product strategy done right.

What changes with GEO and AEO?

Today, the landscape is shifting again. Search is no longer just about “ten blue links.” Generative engines (GEO) and answer engines (AEO) are becoming new front doors for discovery. The practice page tracks that shift.

The fundamentals haven’t changed. You still need to deliver content that speaks to user needs and intent. What’s changed is how and where that value is surfaced.

GEO and AEO often result in zero-click experiences. On the surface, that feels like a loss. Fewer visits. Less top-of-funnel traffic. But here’s the tradeoff: the clicks you do earn become more qualified. They arrive already familiar with your brand. They arrive with context. They arrive with trust.

That’s brand reach. That’s authority. That’s exposure at scale, even if the metrics look different.

We have been living with zero-click disruption in SEO for years. Featured snippets. Knowledge panels. Direct answers.

What’s happening now is just an extension. And the right way to approach it is the same as before:

  • Focus on outcomes, not volume
  • Build for clarity, not clutter
  • Think of brand reach, not just traffic

The traffic you lose at the top gets replaced by stronger, more qualified engagement downstream.

What never changes?

SEO. GEO. AEO. The acronyms change. The fundamentals do not.

  • Understand your audience’s intent
  • Deliver content and experiences that actually solve their problems
  • Build trust through usability, speed, and clarity

If you do that, the platforms, whether search, generative, or answer, will reward you. Not because of metadata. But because people engage. They stay. They trust what you built.

So when I get introduced as the “SEO guy,” I don’t shrug it off anymore.

Because if being the SEO guy means I understand users, systems, stacks, and growth, that’s not a limitation. That’s the whole job.

Thanks for reading,
