Alignment Isn’t a Meeting. It’s a System.

My first Middle Seat essay, from June 2025, and still the operating principle: alignment is not buy-in, and it is not a meeting. It is a system, built through proof and momentum. This edition lives next to the record it came from.

By Douglas Cirillo · First published on Substack, June 3, 2025 · Site edition, November 2025

What does durable alignment look like?

I used to think alignment was about buy-in.

If you got the right people in the room, presented a compelling roadmap, and walked out with nodding heads, you were good to go.

But I’ve lived through enough high-stakes rollouts to know better.

Alignment isn’t agreement. It’s durability.

It’s not a moment. It’s a system.

And most teams don’t build one.

At Sotheby’s, I walked into a 300-year-old brand with a clear mandate from the board and CEO:

Rebuild the business into a modern digital marketplace.

The vision was bold. The support was real.

But everything underneath was fragmented: tools, goals, even language.

Editorial had one set of KPIs.

Marketing had another.

Product was somewhere else entirely.

There was no shared view of the customer journey.

And worse, no trust between teams.

That’s when I learned:

Good alignment doesn’t start with consensus. It starts with proof.

Where does alignment actually start?

I started with a team that was ready to move: digital marketing.

They’d stitched together their own CRM stack, not perfect, but promising.

We built from there.

  • Deployed a CDP tied to real user behavior
  • Synced downstream to Zaius and Adobe
  • Defined shared KPIs around engagement, registration, and bidding intent

I introduced Amplitude, Optimizely, and Algolia, not just as tools, but as ways to listen better and react faster.

We didn’t try to convince everyone all at once.

We shipped. We showed value. Then we invited others in.

Editorial began using SEO data to shape content.

Marketing personalized outreach based on real behavior.

Email volume dropped 50%. Engagement doubled.

And trust started to compound.

How is alignment built?

It’s never a kickoff deck.

It’s never a one-pager.

It’s built through:

  • Shared outcomes, not just shared infrastructure
  • Transparent feedback and fast iteration
  • Respect for where each team is starting, not where you wish they were
  • Systems that continue to work, even when no one’s watching

Does the pattern hold everywhere?

At Lindblad, A Place for Mom, and now in consulting work with clients like A+E, this pattern holds:

You don’t align through convincing. You align through momentum.

You show progress in one place.

Then you expand the surface area of belief.

You create value in motion.

And along the way, you define what “good” actually looks like:

  • One set of metrics that matter
  • One shared understanding of the customer
  • A system that teams can trust and act on without translation

That’s alignment. Not agreement.

Not buy-in.

A shared foundation that survives beyond the meeting.

You don’t just check a box and move on.

You revisit it. Reinforce it. Rebuild it as the business scales.

Because alignment isn’t a milestone.

It’s a system.

That system is the heart of my digital experience strategy practice.

And it’s your job to design it.
