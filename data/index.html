Your data

Your data on this site

What this site captures when you visit, what it does not capture, who processes it, how long it is kept, and what you can do about it. Plain terms, no legal fog.

What does this site capture?

Amplitude measures visits: the pages you view, the session itself, clicks on links that leave the site, and how the conversation and the scorecard are used. Events carry a device ID assigned at random to this browser, and a bot classification. No name and no email is attached. This site never asks for either. For visitors in the European Union, the European Economic Area, or the United Kingdom, measurement does not start until they answer the prompt described under your choices below. Everywhere else, it starts with the visit. Guides on this site also run through Amplitude. The scorecard checklist records which steps you finished, never your individual answers. Guides on this site are demonstrations: visitors are assigned one experience at a time, the assignment is stored on their device and counted in Amplitude, and guides never ask for personal information. The scorecard promotion card is an experiment: visitors are assigned one of two versions at random, and the assignment is counted in Amplitude. If you answer a survey here, your answers go to Amplitude and are read by Douglas. Surveys never ask for your name or email. If a request to the site's own services fails, that failure is recorded, with the status and where the request was headed, so errors can be found and fixed. How fast the pages load and respond is also measured: load time, layout stability, and responsiveness, recorded as numbers only, never anything about you or what you typed.

For a visit the page classifies as human, Amplitude also records the session as a replay: the page as you saw it, clicks and scrolling, console output and failed requests included, so a visit that goes wrong can be watched instead of reconstructed from its events. A failed request is recorded as its address and status only, never its contents. Text typed into a field is masked while it sits in the field. Once a question or an answer is on the page, a replay can show it, the way it shows any other text here. Sessions classified as bots or scanners are never recorded. Where the prompt described below applies, the replay waits for the same yes as the rest of measurement.

Ask the chat a question and it goes to a server to be answered. A Claude model at Anthropic picks the published records that answer it and drafts the answer from them, checked in code against those records before it can serve. A log keeps the day, the page you asked from, your question, the answer it got, and which records were used. Email addresses and phone numbers are stripped automatically before anything is stored. Names and other details are not stripped, so leave them out. The log holds no IP address and no visitor ID. Each entry expires after 90 days, and the table removes expired entries on its own.

Searches run on Algolia. What you type into the search box goes there to be answered. Result views and clicks go to Algolia under the same device ID Amplitude uses, or a random per-session ID when that is not available, so search behavior and site behavior can be read together. My own measurement records a search’s length and result count, never its text. Algolia handles the searches it receives under its own terms.

The site also keeps a small ledger in your own browser: the pages and records you have opened, the records the chat returned for you, the kinds of answers it gave, the intents you picked, and the views you built or looked at. It shapes the conversation when you come back. It never leaves your browser, and it never holds the words you typed. The conversation itself sits in this tab’s session storage and is gone when you close the tab.

What does this site not do?

No accounts. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to sign in to.

No email collection. Nothing on this site asks for your email address. The scorecard runs in your browser and stores nothing you enter.

No advertising trackers. No ad pixels, no data brokers, nothing built to follow you to other sites for ads.

I do not sell data. Nothing captured here is sold, and I do not share it for another company’s marketing.

Who processes it?

GitHub Pages hosts the site and serves the pages.

Amplitude measures the visits and records the session replays.

Amazon Web Services runs the chat’s server, and keeps the question log and a monthly counter of chat calls.

Anthropic provides the Claude model that reads a chat question and picks the records that answer it.

Algolia runs the search. It receives what you type into the search box and the result clicks, under the device ID described above.

Embedded videos are served by YouTube (Google) on its privacy-enhanced domain. Pressing play talks to YouTube, and how much of a video is watched is measured into Amplitude like the rest of the visit.

Any service your browser talks to directly sees your IP address, as on any website. GitHub Pages, Amplitude, Algolia, and YouTube are the ones here.

How long is it kept?

The question log: each entry expires after 90 days, and the table removes expired entries on its own.

Entries in the ledger in your browser stop counting after 90 days at most and are deleted on a later visit. You can clear the ledger sooner, at any time.

The conversation is gone when you close the tab.

Session replays are kept for 30 days, then Amplitude deletes them.

Measurement events have no retention period set in Amplitude today, and no date is set for their deletion.

GitHub Pages, Algolia, and Anthropic keep their own logs under their own terms. Those periods are not listed here.

What choices do you have?

If your browser’s time zone places you in the European Union, the European Economic Area, or the United Kingdom, the site asks before it keeps the ledger or measures the visit. When a browser gives no usable time zone, its language stands in for the time zone in that check. The prompt says: “This site can remember what you have opened here, in your own browser, and let it shape the conversation when you come back. That memory never leaves this browser. It can also measure the visit with Amplitude: the pages you view, and the session recorded as a replay, under a device ID assigned at random, with no name or email attached. Both stay off until you answer. Keep it?” Until you answer, the ledger stays empty, Amplitude measures nothing, and no session is recorded. Say no, by the button or by closing the prompt, and they stay off on later visits too, until you change your mind. Say yes, and they run. Everywhere else, the ledger and measurement are on by default, and no prompt appears.

If the prompt applies to you, the panel that shows the ledger is also where you change your mind. A yes can be withdrawn there: measurement and replay stop from that point in the session, and stay off on later visits. A no can be reversed the same way. Withdrawing a yes does not delete events already recorded. Replays already recorded are deleted after 30 days. For the rest, use the email route below.

Wherever you are visiting from, you can open the ledger, read exactly what it holds, remove a category, or clear it entirely.

Clearing the ledger does not change the device ID this browser uses for measurement, and it does not delete measurement events already recorded. To reset the device ID itself, clear this site’s data in your browser settings. For events already recorded, use the email route below.

The conversation has its own control, Clear the conversation, next to Exit. It empties what this tab holds.

To see what is held about your visit, or to have it deleted, email me at douglas.cirillo@gmail.com. Say roughly when you visited and what you did here. The device ID is all that ties measurement to a browser, so the date and the shape of the visit are what make a match possible, and a match can be approximate. Chat questions cannot be matched to anyone: the log holds no identifier, and its entries expire after 90 days on their own. Requests are answered within 30 days.
