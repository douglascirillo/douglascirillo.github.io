How this site runs

Douglas Cirillo is Head of Digital Experience and Growth at Vanguard. This page is the colophon for his personal site: the stack it runs on, how it is measured, and the team and rules behind it.

This site is a working demo. It is built and run the way I say digital teams should work: a small stack, data first, and a human making the decisions.

What is it built on?

Hand-written HTML. No framework, no CMS, no build step. Every page’s content is in the HTML itself, readable with JavaScript off. JavaScript layers on top for the interactive parts: the record bar, the dynamic lists, and the compositions. Lists whose entries lead to pages of their own render as preview cards by default: image where the page has one, tag, title, summary, and the read call. Reference lists inside a page stay text. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages and every change is a git commit, so the full history of what changed and when is versioned like software. Because it is software.

How is it measured?

Amplitude has measured the site since day one: page views, sessions, and outbound clicks. Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools watch indexing, and Bing matters more than it used to because its index feeds AI answers. Semrush tracks a short list of keywords, my name and the ideas I write about. A personal site gets the same measurement discipline as a business property. Every event also carries a bot classification, an is_bot flag and a user_agent_type category (human, search crawler, AI crawler, social preview, uptime monitor, scanner, unknown) parsed from the user agent at load, so the reporting can filter to humans and break the automated traffic down by kind.

Who helps run it?

I work with a small bench of AI systems, each with a lane. Claude pressure-tests positioning. Grok checks claims against what is live right now. Gemini works through long documents and diligence. ChatGPT argues the other side. An AI agent coordinates the day-to-day: research, drafts, publishing, and regular checks on how the site and channels are doing.

Every draft comes back to me. I edit, I decide, I approve. Nothing publishes on a model’s say-so.

How does the site adapt?

The home page asks what brought you here and routes you to the part of the record that answers it. The leadership scorecard runs as a self-diagnostic in your browser; nothing you enter is stored. Underneath, every claim on the site sits in a structured registry, record.json, with its source and status, published for answer engines alongside an llms.txt summary. The pages themselves stay static and crawlable. The record does not change. How you move through it does.

How does Ask my record work?

Every page on this site has a question box. It answers from one source: a public evidence registry, record.json, that holds every claim on this site as data, word for word, with its source page. The registry is the site, in machine-readable form.

A small AI model (Claude Haiku) reads the question and picks which records answer it. The model never writes the answer. Code assembles the reply from the registry verbatim and links the source. Numbers cannot drift, because the model never touches them. If no record answers, the box says so and points to the case studies. If a question is out of bounds (my employer beyond my published role, compensation, my family), it gets one fixed refusal, every time. Some exclusions are enforced in code, not just in instructions to the model.

There is one input, at the top of every page, and it is also the search box (next section). Ask a question and the answer renders right there, in place. The box knows which page you are on. It suggests questions that page can actually answer, prefers that page’s records when several could answer, and ends with the next step that fits the page. The nav’s Ask link just focuses the input. It runs on a serverless function (AWS Lambda) that I deployed from a script, and it is measured like everything else here: views, questions, answers, and next clicks in Amplitude.

Questions are kept for 90 days so I can read what people actually ask and improve the record. Email addresses and phone numbers are stripped automatically before anything is stored. The question log keeps no visitor identifiers. After 90 days the log deletes itself.

Cost is capped twice: a monthly ceiling in the code, and a spend limit where the model bill actually lands. There is a kill switch on the site side and on the server side.

Before any of this shipped, it had to pass a 20-case test set: ordinary questions, scope traps, and adversarial ones. The first run passed 17. The three failures were fixed in the open: a missing record, a selection preference, and an exclusion that needed code instead of instructions. The set has to pass 20 of 20 before changes go live.

The walk-away pair, side by side. A walk-away test asks whether the bar declines what it cannot verify. The pair runs it both ways: the same subject asked twice, once where the fact is not published and once where it is. Both answers below are verbatim from the live bar, and the pair now runs in the test set, which stands at 22 cases.

Question: “How many people were on the Sotheby’s team?”
Answer: “I don’t have published evidence for that. The full record is on this site, and the case studies are the fastest way through it.”

No Sotheby’s team size is published, so the bar names what it cannot verify and stops. It does not borrow the Lindblad team of 10. That fact is published, but it belongs to a different question.

Question: “What organic search growth did the Sotheby’s work produce?”
Answer: “Organic traffic at Sotheby’s rose 167% during Douglas Cirillo’s tenure leading the digital product and growth stack.” (Sotheby’s case study)

That fact is published, so the bar answers with the claim exactly as written and links its source. Nothing was added to the record for this test. Only the questions changed.

Build your own. Publish your record as data, with sources. Keep every claim verbatim. Let the model select, never write. Write your refusals in advance and make them fixed. Log questions with a short fuse. Cap the spend where the bill lands. Gate changes on a test set that includes people trying to break it.

How does search work?

The same input searches the site. It runs on Algolia, the on-site search and recommendations tool from my Sotheby’s stack, over a public index built from the published pages: titles, fixed summaries, headings. Nothing else. Restricted records never enter it, and results show the fixed summary rather than a raw snippet, so a figure cannot be separated from its scope. The index rebuilds on every publish, into a temporary index that swaps in only on success, so the site and the index never disagree.

Type a few words, get pages. Ask a full question, and the input routes to Ask My Record instead. Results lean toward the kind of page you are already on.

The writing list on the home page and the related essays under each article are served from the same index. Publish a new essay and the index rebuilds itself, so the lists update on their own. Lists show the three most relevant items, with a link through to the full list.

The one-line introductions above the writing lists are AI-generated. Each is written ahead of time from the listed essays’ own summaries, checked against them before use, and labeled. If a check fails, the list appears without one.

Search queries are measured by length and result count, never stored as text. Result clicks are sent to Algolia under the same anonymous ID that Amplitude uses, so I can read search behavior and site behavior together. No names, no emails, nothing that identifies a person.

How do the key points work?

Most articles and case studies on this site open with a Key points box. It is the second generated surface here, after the one-line introductions above the writing lists, and the first one built to be quoted: each set is written ahead of time from the page’s own text and nothing else, so an answer engine citing a line is citing the page itself.

Two checks run before any set is used. A mechanical check rejects a set when a number, a name, or a capitalized phrase in it does not appear in the page. A second model then reads for the drift a mechanical check cannot see: a figure detached from its scope, a role upgraded beyond what the page says, a cause the page never claimed, the author’s name repeated until a summary reads like a bio. A set that fails either check is discarded, and a page with no passing set carries no box. All twenty articles and case studies carry one today. The boxes are labeled AI-generated, and they are part of the indexable page on purpose.

How does the writing work?

Essays publish first on Substack, where the discussion lives. Each one also gets a redrafted edition here under Writing: a new framing against the record, question-shaped headings, and links into the case studies and practice pages. Never a verbatim copy. The site edition is the version built to be found. Substack keeps the conversation.

How do the record pages work?

Each record page is a frozen composition: blocks of approved text, assembled once and published as a static page. The proof blocks trace to the case studies, and the figures carry their scope with them. Nothing on these pages is generated at visit time. The writing lists update as new essays are approved. The proof does not change without re-approval.

How do compositions work?

Pick a problem on the home page and the site assembles the full picture for it on the spot: the intent frame, the proof, and the practice behind it, drawn from the same approved blocks as the record pages. The set of possible assemblies is fixed in advance and checked by a validator before any of them can serve. A link can carry a composition, the address ends in a short code, but it cannot invent one: an unrecognized code serves the ordinary page. Nothing is written at visit time, and switching compositions off changes nothing underneath.

How is the site put together?

Three layers, one rule: a model can select and arrange. It cannot invent.

The record layer is static: plain HTML pages and one data file, record.json, holding every approved claim with its source. GitHub Pages serves them, and every change is a git commit.

The retrieval layer answers. Keyword searches go to an Algolia index built only from the published pages. Questions go to a small function on AWS: a Claude model picks the records that answer, and code assembles the reply from those records word for word. Nothing in the answer is written by the model.

The generative layer is narrow on purpose. The introductions over the writing lists are written ahead of time by an offline script, from the listed essays’ own summaries. A mechanical checker and a second model review each line before it is stored, and the site serves the stored file. Nothing is generated while a visitor waits.

Measurement runs through Amplitude, joined to Algolia under one anonymous ID, so search behavior and site behavior can be read together. No names, no emails. Each layer can be switched off on its own; the static record serves either way.

What are the rules?

Settled facts only. Every number on this site traces to work I actually did. One change at a time, then measure. And the harness checks from my writing, Brand Out-of-Bounds, Contextual Switch, and Guardrails, apply to the team that builds this site too. Gates, not prompts.

The argument of my writing is that a small team with a real foundation outruns a big team on a bloated stack. This site is that argument, running in production.

Where is this going?

The record does not change. What changes is how much of the experience around it can assemble itself, under the same gates. The set lives in the record.

Next

Ask gains connective writing between the records it cites, after a review period of real visitor questions, with the verbatim answer kept as the fallback. The connective writing is built and tested; the review period is what remains.

Then: composition

Pages that assemble around a visitor’s intent from the approved block library, precomputed and checked the way the introductions are checked. The static pages stay underneath as the canonical record, and any composition can be switched off without touching them.
